Based on the retrieved papers, PD-L1 levels can be a useful predictor of response to immunotherapy in solid tumors, particularly in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Studies have shown that tumors with high PD-L1 expression are more likely to respond to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). [1]


However, PD-L1 levels alone may not be sufficient to predict response. A study integrating clinical, genomic, and survival data of 287 patients with advanced NSCLC found that genomic correlates — such as tumor mutation burden — were also important predictors of response. [2] This suggests that a more comprehensive approach, combining multiple biomarkers and clinical factors, may be necessary to accurately predict treatment efficacy.


In summary, while PD-L1 levels are a valuable biomarker for immunotherapy response prediction, they work best as part of a broader diagnostic panel rather than as a standalone marker.

⚠ The retrieved papers do not directly address the treatment of refractory autoimmune encephalitis.

Autoimmune encephalitis is a condition where the immune system mistakenly attacks the brain, leading to inflammation and damage. Treatment typically involves immunosuppressive therapies to reduce inflammation. Rituximab, a monoclonal antibody targeting B cells, has been used in some related conditions — though its efficacy in refractory cases is unclear. [1]


Biologic therapies such as tocilizumab, which targets interleukin-6, have also been explored in cytokine-driven conditions. However, it is unclear whether these therapies are effective in refractory autoimmune encephalitis specifically. [3]


Unfortunately, the available retrieved literature does not provide sufficient information to make specific recommendations for this condition. Further research is needed.
