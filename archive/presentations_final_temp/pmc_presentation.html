We built and evaluated a system that reads 10,000 papers and writes a synthesized answer to any medical question in seconds.
The Problem
35 million papers. Zero synthesis.
Why search alone is not enough
  • PubMed returns links — not answers. Clinicians must read and synthesize themselves.
  • Keyword search misses papers that use different terminology for the same concept.
  • New evidence accumulates faster than anyone can keep up with.
  • Time pressure in clinical settings makes deep literature review impractical.
  • No standard tool connects a specific question to a synthesized, evidence-backed answer.
The gap in practice
"I searched PubMed for an hour and found 340 results. None of them gave me a clear answer on first-line treatment. I ended up calling a colleague."
— Composite from physician interviews on clinical literature search
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) closes this gap: retrieve the most relevant text from a corpus, then have a language model synthesize a direct answer grounded in those sources.
Clinicians need synthesized answers, not ranked lists of links. RAG makes that possible at scale.

What's Next
Where this goes from here
01
Re-embed with clean data
We found that reference list text was contaminating the FAISS embeddings. Re-running the index with abstract + introduction only takes ~30 minutes on a T4 GPU and is the single highest-leverage fix available right now.
02
Expand corpus coverage
Q7 and Q8 scored low because those topics are simply not in the corpus. Adding ophthalmology and neonatology articles would directly improve those answers — no model changes needed at all.
03
Human expert evaluation
AI-judge ratings are a useful proxy, but the next step is domain experts rating the same 24 answers — and eventually testing whether the system's answers influence real clinical decisions differently.
04
Deploy as a proper tool
The core architecture is production-ready. Capstone II will wrap it in a proper interface, add transparency (which papers support which claim), and evaluate on a broader, more diverse question set.
Cleaner embeddings and a broader corpus are the fastest path to better results — before any model upgrades.
